## Offer a choice as its consequences

Whenever you put more than one way forward in front of the user — two readings of their intent, two placements, a layer or a module, a merge or a split — give each option **1–2 sentences of its consequence on the grid**:

- which modules, layer sections and `<index>` rows change;
- which dependency records appear or disappear;
- what a coding agent will receive in a brief;
- what is hard to undo later — a module number, a layer convention N modules fill, a split module file;
- what gets deferred.

Close with **a recommendation and one sentence of why**. The user may overrule it; they must not have to reconstruct it.

Not this: *"Should this be a layer or part of M03?"* — with nothing after it. The user rarely knows the grid as well as you do; a bare choice hands them your work.

This:

> *"As a layer L2 — M03, M05 and M07 each get a new section to fill from its schema, and a later change to the convention touches all three. As M03's `## Domain` — one place to edit, but M05 and M07 gain a dependency record on M03. I'd go with M03: only M03 owns the behaviour today; the other two just read it."*

One option only → no ceremony: state its consequence in one line inside the confirmation.
